Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Dothan: What to Know

A leaking pipe rarely announces itself. Water seeps out behind drywall, at the back of a sink cabinet or under the concrete until a stain, a soft floor or an unexplained water bill gives it away. By then the materials around the pipe have often been wet for weeks. We serve Dothan homeowners by tracing the full wet area and drying every bit of it, including what is out of sight.

A plumber can stop the drip, though the water that soaked into the structure is still there afterward. Getting it out is the work we do. With meters and a thermal camera we locate every wet material, make only the openings required, then keep drying equipment running until moisture levels read normal again. A slow leak gives mold plenty of time to begin, so we inspect for growth too and treat any surface it has reached.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Stop the Water SupplyFor a leak at a toilet or sink, turn off the small supply valve feeding that fixture. When the source is unclear, the house main is the valve to turn off.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityIf water is near outlets, switches or a light fixture, switch off the breaker for that area. Leave any wet cord or electrical device alone.
  3. Read the MeterShut off all taps and fixtures, then go look at the water meter. If the dial is still moving, a pipe is leaking somewhere you cannot see, possibly under the slab.
  4. Clear Out and Take PhotosEmpty the closet or cabinet by the leak, then photograph the pipe, any stains and any warped material before repairs start or items are discarded.
  5. Get a Moisture CheckHave the area measured with meters before it is closed up. Drywall can feel dry to the touch while the cavity behind it is still damp.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Years of excessive water pressure straining valves and joints until one of them starts to weep.
  • Drain fittings and P-traps below sinks that work loose and leak whenever water goes down.
  • Loose or worn compression fittings and supply connections under sinks and behind toilets.
  • Water lines under a concrete slab that get rubbed through or stressed as the soil moves.
  • A crimp ring or solder joint that gives out inside a ceiling or wall cavity where nobody can see it.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Stained Drywall and Ceilings

A pipe leaking above a ceiling or inside a wall leaves brown rings and bubbled paint. If the board keeps taking on water it loses its stiffness, starts to sag and may have to be removed.

Swollen Cabinets

Sink cabinet bottoms are usually particleboard, which swells and crumbles when it soaks up a leak. Because the board never shrinks back, a long leak often ends with a new cabinet base.

Flooring Damage

A leak in a wall or under the slab sends water beneath the floor covering. Laminate seams swell, hardwood boards cup, and the glue holding tile or vinyl releases.

Mold Inside the Wall

Mold thrives in a wall cavity that is both damp and dark. With a slow leak it often covers the back of the drywall before a single spot shows on the painted side.

Insurance

What About Insurance?

A slow leak and a sudden one are not handled the same way by insurance. Coverage is common for accidental, sudden damage, while a leak that ran for months or weeks is usually not covered and is specifically excluded in some policies. An adjuster may look more kindly on a leak hidden from view than on one that could be seen and was left alone. Check your policy and ask your agent. So the adjuster can work from solid facts, we provide photos, moisture readings and notes on the leak source.
